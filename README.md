# Dress-Up Learning Games

Touch-and-drag learning games for preschoolers. Things she can change sit right in the picture for her to drag, the buttons are big and chunky, and the game says every color, shape, number and letter out loud.

**▶ Play Zahra's Dress-Up: https://wkotlewski.github.io/Dress-Up-Learning-Games-/**

## Zahra's Dress-Up (`index.html`)

A dress-up game for a girl turning 4 who is learning colors, shapes, numbers, how to spell her name, and good manners.

### One step at a time

Each screen shows only the next choice, and the game reads it out loud. A big **Back** button and a **Home** button are always at the top, and the top bar shows where she is.

1. **Where are we going today?** She picks a place.
2. **In that place**, she gets dressed in a matching outfit. She can go on that place's **adventure**, or choose what to change: tops, bottoms, shoes, hats, and so on. Only the clothes that fit that place show up.
3. **Pick a top** puts the tops on a shelf under the girl. She drags one onto the girl to put it on.
4. **Drag a color onto her!** puts paint pots on both sides of the girl and patterns on the shelf. She drags a pot or a pattern onto the girl. Then she taps **Done**.

She can also tap the girl herself: her head for hats, her chest for tops, her legs for bottoms, her feet for shoes, her face for makeup, or her hands to go to the nail salon.

### Playing vs. answering

It's always clear whether she's playing or being asked something:

- **Playing:** when she's dressing up, doing makeup, cleaning up, or on an adventure, the picture takes over the screen. The words sit in a slim strip above it, and the few buttons she needs (like **Done**) float on the picture. On a tablet or computer, the scene widens to fill the whole screen instead of sitting in a narrow box.
- **Answering:** a question takes over the whole screen with a bright **Question time!** banner and big answer buttons. When she answers, she goes right back to the picture.

She's free to explore. Nothing flashes "do this next" while she's busy. Things she can drag give a small wiggle now and then, so she knows they move. If she stops for a few seconds, a soft glow shows what to try next. In an adventure, when she finishes an activity (splashing, dancing, kicking goals), she can keep playing as long as she wants and taps **Next** when she's ready.

### Drag it on, drag it off

She changes things by moving them in the picture, with very few buttons:

- **Clothes:** drag from the shelf onto the girl to put it on. To take something off, grab it on the girl (her hat, her top, her shoes) and drag it away.
- **Colors and patterns:** drag a paint pot or a pattern onto her.
- **Makeup:** paint pots sit beside her face. She drags one onto her face for lips, cheeks or eye shadow, or drags a tattoo onto her face right where she wants it.
- **Clean-up:** she drags each toy into the toy box.
- **Adventures:** she drags the boots, helmet, backpack or slippers onto the girl, and drags them off her at the end.
- **Nail salon:** she drags the polish bottle or the nail file onto a nail, and the lotion onto her hands.

While she's dragging, a dashed yellow outline shows where to drop it. If she lets go somewhere else, it goes back. Tapping still works everywhere too.

### Places and adventures

Each place has an adventure: a few scenes in a row where she makes the girl do things. Dots show how far along she is. She plays it like a game, in two ways:

- **Touch things in the picture:** the boots to put them on, a puddle to jump in, the bus to get on, a candle to blow out, the ball to bounce it. If she stops for a few seconds, a glowing ring shows what to tap next.
- **Game buttons on the picture**, named for that scene: **Walk** and **Jump** on the rainy sidewalk, **Pedal** and **Ring bell** on the bike, **Drive** and **Honk** on the bus, **Get on the bus**, **Go in school**, **Raise hand**, **Pick up** for shells, **Kick**, **Plié**, **Blow** and so on. Hold **Walk** (or Pedal or Drive) to keep moving, and **Back** to go back. At the stop sign the arrow buttons become **Look left** and **Look right**. On a computer, the arrow keys and the space bar work too.

| Place | Clothes | Adventure |
|---|---|---|
| Rainy day | raincoat, rain boots, rain hat, umbrella | **Puddle walk:** put on rain boots → walk down the sidewalk and jump in each muddy puddle → home, take off the muddy boots and put them on the mat |
| School day | name shirt, skirt, sneakers, backpack | **School day:** put on the backpack → the bus pulls up, tap to get on → say "Good morning!" to the driver → ride to school → go in school → sit at a desk and raise your hand to answer three questions → ride home → say "Thank you!" to the driver → hang up the backpack |
| Sunny park | T-shirt, shorts, cap, sunglasses, bubble wand | **Bike to the park:** put on a helmet → ride the bike, stop at the stop sign and look left and right → helmets off on the playground → go down the slide three times → helmet back on → ride home safely |
| Fancy party | party dress, ball gown, tiara, pearls, purse | **The party:** walk in and catch a balloon → dance → blow out four candles on the cake → say "Thank you for the party!" |
| Ballet show | leotard, tutu, tights, ballet slippers | **Ballet class:** put on slippers → hold the barre and plié four times → twirl in the middle → take a bow while flowers fly → take off the slippers and put them in the bag |
| Soccer practice | jersey with a number, shorts, soccer socks, cleats | **Soccer practice:** put on cleats → warm up running around the cones → kick three goals → say "Good game!" → take off the cleats |
| Bedtime | pajamas, nightgown, slippers, sleepy cap, sleep mask, teddy | **Bedtime:** bath (pop six bubbles) → brush teeth while counting to ten → put on pajamas → read a story with Teddy (the pages count 1–5 and spell Z‑A‑H‑R‑A) → lights off, good night |
| Beach | swimsuit, swim ring, flip-flops, sun hat, sand bucket | **Beach day:** walk to the water picking up shells → splash in the water → walk back to the sand → bounce the beach ball → build a sandcastle one piece at a time |
| My room | every piece of clothing in the game | Dance · Twirl |

There's also **Makeup** (lip gloss, cheeks, eye shadow, sticker tattoos) and **Me** (skin tone, hairstyle, hair color). She starts as a mixed-race girl with brown skin and dark curly hair.

### Stars and prizes

This works like the Prize Shop in Star Knight League, made for a toddler. She earns **stars**:

- 1 star for each right answer
- 2 stars for cleaning up
- 3 stars for finishing at the nail salon
- 5 stars for each adventure, and 10 the first time she finishes it

There's a daily limit of 30 stars, which you can change. Her stars show in the corner. Tapping them opens a **star jar** that fills toward the next prize, and a **prize list** with pictures:

| Prize | Stars |
|---|---|
| Stickers | 10 |
| Bubble bath | 15 |
| Extra bedtime story | 20 |
| Trip to the park | 30 |
| Ice cream | 40 |
| Pick a movie | 50 |
| New toy | 80 |
| Playdate with a friend | 100 |

When she has enough stars, she taps a prize and answers **"Yes, please!"** The stars are used up, and she gets a **certificate** with her picture to show a grown-up. Past prizes stay under **My prizes**. In the grown-ups corner you can rename prizes, change their star costs and pictures, add or remove prizes, give or take away stars, set the daily limit, and check off prizes once she's received them.

### Nail salon

When she taps **Nails**, the game asks if she wants to go to the nail salon. There, Nina the nail artist asks what to do next, and Zahra chooses: soak (pop the bubbles), shape (short, long, square or pointy "fake" nails), polish, sparkles, dry (count to five), lotion, or hand tattoos. Each step she finishes gets a checkmark.

### Learning and manners

- **Questions** pop up before something new goes on and between adventure scenes: "Which one is pink?", "How many stars?", "Find the number 7!", "Find the letter B!", "What letter does Moon start with?", "Red, blue, red, blue… what comes next?", "Which one is big?", "Which one has more?", "Which one is the heart?", "Z, A, H… what comes next?" She can tap the answer or press **Say it!** and say it out loud. A wrong answer tells her what she picked and lets her try again.
- **Please and thank you:** at the salon, Nina offers each step and Zahra answers **"Yes, please!"** or **"No, thank you."** She finishes each step with **"Thank you, Nina!"** and Nina says "You're welcome!"
- **Clean-up time:** after she plays somewhere, leaving brings up the toys on the floor. She drags each one into the toy box (or taps it), counting as she goes, "so we don't lose them."

### Your voice

In the grown-ups corner, **Record my voice** lists about 60 short phrases: her name, cheers ("Yes!", "Good job!"), the 10 colors, numbers one to ten, the letters Z, A, H, R, shapes, common questions, please and thank-you phrases, and bedtime lines like "Good night, Zahra" and "I love you." Tap the red button, say it, and tap again. Record as many or as few as you like.

Whenever the game says a word or phrase you recorded, it plays your voice, even in the middle of a sentence. "Which one is pink?" plays your "Which one is" and your "Pink." The built-in voice fills in everything else. Recordings stay in that browser on that device, so record on the iPad she plays on.

**Voice type** lets you choose any English voice the device has, set the pitch lower, middle or higher and the speed slow, normal or quick, and tap **Hear it** to try it.

### Grown-ups corner

**Press and hold** the gear button for a second and a half to open it, like Star Knight League. A quick tap only shows "Grown-ups: press and hold", so she can't wander in. It has:

- **How she's doing:** for each skill, how often she's right on the first try, what she's doing great at and what needs practice, plus a list of recently missed questions.
- **Questions:** how often they pop up (off, some, lots, or every time), the level (easier: numbers 1 to 5, or turning 4: 1 to 10), and which skills to practice: colors, counting, numbers, shapes, spelling ZAHRA, letters, first-letter sounds, patterns, big and small, and more or fewer. Questions come up when she puts something on, between adventure scenes, and in class.
- **Stars and prizes:** give stars (+1, +5, +10, +20, +50), take some away or set them to 0, make her earn double or triple stars, set the daily limit (20, 30, 50, 100 or none), edit the prize list (names, costs, pictures), restore the default prizes, clear the prizes she's gotten, and check off prizes once she has them.
- **Clean-up time** on or off.
- **Talking and your voice:** talking on or off, record your voice, voice type, pitch and speed.
- **Reset** (each one asks you to tap twice): her clothes, makeup and nails; stars and prizes; the learning tracker; or everything.

Her outfit, stars and prizes are saved in the browser between visits. Answering out loud needs a browser that supports speech recognition, like Chrome, or Safari on iPhone and iPad. It asks for the microphone the first time.

### Run it

Play it at **https://wkotlewski.github.io/Dress-Up-Learning-Games-/** on any phone, tablet or computer. On an iPad or iPhone, tap Share → Add to Home Screen to give it its own icon.

The link works once GitHub Pages is on for this repo (Settings → Pages → deploy from `main`, root folder). The game is a single HTML file with no build step, so you can also open `index.html` straight in a browser.
